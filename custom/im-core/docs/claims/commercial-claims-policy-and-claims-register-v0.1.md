## 1. Purpose

This document defines the commercial claims policy and claims register for OpenEMR IM Core.

The purpose is to prevent unsupported clinical, regulatory, security, commercial, production, AI, medical-device, and performance claims from being made before appropriate evidence, review, and gate approval exist.

This document is documentation-stage only.

Golden rule:

The system must support clinicians. It must not replace clinicians.

## 5. Claims Policy Principle

Every claim must be:

- Accurate.
- Evidence-linked.
- Reviewable.
- Bounded.
- Stage-appropriate.
- Free of unsupported readiness language.
- Free of exaggerated clinical, regulatory, security, commercial, or AI capability language.

A claim must not exceed the current evidence.

If evidence does not exist, the claim must not be made.

## 17. Claims Review Workflow

A future claim should follow this workflow:

1. Draft claim.
2. Identify audience.
3. Identify claim category.
4. Link evidence.
5. Link risk if applicable.
6. Review limitations.
7. Assign required reviewers.
8. Approve, reject, revise, or retire.
9. Record in claims register.
10. Review periodically.

No claim should be published externally before review.

## 22. Correction and Retraction

If an unsupported claim is discovered:

1. Stop use of the claim.
2. Identify where the claim appears.
3. Notify appropriate owner.
4. Correct or remove the claim.
5. Document the correction.
6. Update claims register if needed.
7. Review whether risk register update is needed.
8. Prevent recurrence through checklist or template updates.

---

## 23. Claims Stop Rules

Stop communication, PR preparation, demo preparation, training, support, or external presentation if:

- Unsupported clinical claim appears.
- Unsupported regulatory claim appears.
- Unsupported security/privacy claim appears.
- Unsupported commercial claim appears.
- Unsupported production readiness claim appears.
- Unsupported AI/automation claim appears.
- Claim suggests replacement of clinician judgment.
- Claim suggests clinical validation without evidence.
- Claim suggests compliance or certification without evidence.
- Claim cannot be linked to evidence.
